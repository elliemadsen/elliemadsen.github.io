This computational mapping project employs data analysis and visualization to illustrate Manhattan's spatial and semantic structure and distribution, questioning whether the city's physical form, density, and land use correlate with its qualitative and experiential sense of place. By regionally clustering Manhattan by both spatial and semantic features, this project contrasts the role of distance in embedding space and distance in the real world.

K-means clustering is used to partition city blocks by spatial attributes derived from building and lot data, including density, height, floor area, and land-use intensity, to establish a baseline spatial clustering that reflects conventional urban morphology.

An alternate classification system derived from semantic data was created by embedding text associated with geographical areas—such as business categories, names, and user-generated descriptions—using an open source ML model. This computationally encodes the semantic content of geographic descriptors—in other words, how spaces are experienced and understood. Clustering this semantic space produces a partitioning of Manhattan that captures language and association rather than spatial form.

Contrasting the resulting clustering schemas reveals that certain neighborhoods remain consistent across both approaches, while others diverge. For example, Central Park emerges as a single coherent region in the spatial clustering, grouped with other low-density spaces like Riverside Park and StuyTown. Yet semantically, the park is not uniform; its edges cluster apart from its interior, reflecting distinct spatial associations and cultural meanings. This demonstrates how places that appear spatially homogeneous contain complex semantic identities that depend on immaterial context and proximity.

These relationships are represented by an interactive web map. The blocks in each spatial and semantic cluster are connected to their nearest neighbors in embedding space, forming networks in which edges represent similarity. This interface visualizes intangible relations of meaning and form as an navigable environment.
