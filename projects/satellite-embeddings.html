Critical Atlas of Earth Embeddings is my ongoing Master's thesis for Computational Design Practices at Columbia University Graduate School of Architecture, Preservation, and Planning.

I argue that Earth embeddings are a new type of map that radically redefine space and distance. Therefore the established vocabulary of mapping (projection, perspective, scale, rhetoric and abstraction), as well as the frameworks introduced by satellite imagery (machine vision and planetary surveillance), offer productive approaches to making sense of Earth embedding data. Yet this novel mode of mapping also introduces new spatial conditions — semantics, dimensionality, and global interoperability. I apply computational and conceptual methods from exploratory data analysis to critical cartography in order to situate geospatial embeddings within the disciplines of remote sensing, machine learning, and philosophies of representation.
